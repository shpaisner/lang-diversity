The kilns of Bursley stood up in the dusk, squat bellied bottles of brick, each with its necklace of fire, and William Leadford went under them with his hands in his pockets and his head down, hearing the clogs. Clack, clack, clack. Home from the shift. Marl and slip and saggar-dust. Dust in the throat, dust in the eyes, dust to dust, and the potbank whistle blowing over it all like a cow in labour.

Nettie. Nettie Stanton. A name to put on a plate, in blue, with a willow and two doves and the bridge where the lovers go over and the old father after them with the whip. Ha. He spat in the cut. The canal took it, black and slow, and bore it toward Etruria, where Wedgwood's ghost no doubt sat in a bowler hat counting the cups.

Gone. Gone since Thursday. A note on the dresser under the teapot, her mother's face in the doorway like a boiled suet pudding. Dear Will, don't hate me. Hate her? He hated the very grammar of it. Don't hate me. Do, do, do. He walked and the word walked with him. Verrall. Verrall of the motor and the yellow gloves, the three-guinea tie, whose father's money was in everything, in the coal under your boots and the clay under that, in the very sheets of the bed. Stop. Stop that.

Over the chimney-pots, low in the east, hung a thing not yet a star, a smear, a pinch of pallor like a thumbprint on a windowpane. The comet, they said. Coming. And who was he, a clerk at thirty shillings, to be looked at by it?

She had said he was too serious. On Trentham road, a Sunday, the beeches dripping, her hand in the crook of his arm as light as a glove left on a seat. You're so serious, Will. You never laugh at a thing just to laugh. And he had told her of the Board school and the evening class, of Ruskin in a penny edition, of the day when the world would be taken in hand and shaken, the pots made for the many and the many fed, and she had looked at a hat in a window across the road. A green hat. With a feather like a question.

Oh, he had talked. Wind and water. Mouth full of the future, and meanwhile the present put on its yellow gloves and drove up in a motor, honk, honk, the horn of Jericho, and every wall in Staffordshire down flat.

Where now? A room in London with a lamp the colour of a peach. Her hair let down, that brown that was not brown, the colour of tea when the milk has just touched it. Verrall's hand there. There. The hand with the signet ring, the hand that never lifted a sagger or signed a docket or wrote out in a round clerkly script Received with thanks.

Received with thanks. He would give thanks. He would give, by God, he would give.

His mother's clock struck in the kitchen where his mother was not, being six years under the yews at St John's, and the clock struck on regardless, a thing that could not be told.

At the works next morning Gadsby the foreman had the Signal spread on a crate of jugs, and the men stood round it with their caps pushed back and their tea going cold in the cans.

—Hast sin it, owd lad? Gadsby said. Wednesday it'll be at its nearest. Nowt but a puff o' gas, the paper says, a tail o' nowt, a hundred million mile long and thin as a lady's breath.

—Then what's it fussin' for?

—Ask the Kaiser.

Laughter, a cough, the pug-mill grinding. For it was all in the columns together, comet and Kaiser, the cyanogen and the Dreadnoughts, the Balkans like a pan on the hob, a Frenchman shot, an Austrian note, a Russian pen scratching in a snowy room. Spread out in grey type on the crate of jugs, the whole old world was leaning in toward its own funeral and the men grinning at it, because it was a Tuesday and it was not yet theirs.

Leadford said nothing. He stood with the ledger under his arm and read upside down. Mobilisation. A fine word, sleek and rolling, a word with wheels. Let them come, he thought, let them come with their guns and their long gas, let Berlin and Paris and London pull one another down like drunkards on a stair. Let the pots break. Let Verrall have his commission in a regiment, and may a shell from Krupp's find the exact spot on his waistcoat.

—Leadford! Wake up, mon. Thy face'd turn milk.

—It's turned already, he said.

Three balls hung over Cobridge, the trinity of the poor, and under them a window that held the whole sad estate of man: a concertina, a christening mug, forty wedding rings strung on a bootlace like a rosary of the jilted, and, on a bed of faded plush, the revolver.

The pawnbroker, a small man with an eyeglass and an air of sorrowing over every item, laid it on the counter.

—Webley, he said. Good as the day. Belonged to a captain, a gentleman. Went out to Africa with it, came back without the use of his liver. Sixteen and six, and cartridges a shilling the box.

—I'll take it.

—For the burglars, is it?

—For the burglars.

—Ah, said the pawnbroker, gently, as one who knew all the burglars of Staffordshire by their first names, and every one a sweetheart. He wrapped it in brown paper and tied the string in a bow, a small bow, almost tender, and it was this bow that nearly undid Leadford, standing there with the weight of the thing in his hands. A present. A parcel for a birthday. To Mr. Verrall, with love.

Outside the sky had gone the colour of a bruise on its third day. He felt the parcel against his ribs and walked, and a boy went past whistling "Let Me Like a Soldier Fall," and the comet in the east had grown a little in the hour, a pale lamp left burning in another house.

Night, and the lodging-house ticking. Mrs. Pargeter's stair, Mrs. Pargeter's cat, Mrs. Pargeter's snore going up and coming down like a bucket in a well. Leadford lay with the parcel under the bolster and did not sleep.

The window was open. There it hung, over the slag heap and the roofs, no longer a smear but a thing with a head, a bright head, and behind it, drawn out upon the dark, the faintest mane, as of a horse galloping, galloping, and never moving. Greenish. A greenness not of grass or leaf but of something under water, a bottle held to the sun. A hundred million miles. And he, William, on a flock mattress in Bursley, with sixteen and six of iron against his hair.

What is a man. A clerk, a kiln, a cup. Fired once, glazed, cracked in the cooling. And she, Nettie, in her peach lamp, does she look up, does she say Look, dear, the comet, and Verrall say Yes, rather, and draw the blind?

Draw the blind. Draw the blind. He would draw it, by God, for the two of them.

He rose. Struck a match, and the match was a small yellow lie against that cold green. He put on the collar, the good collar, the one for chapel and funerals. A change of linen into a bag. Eleven shillings and fourpence on the washstand, the savings of a lifetime, the price of a ticket to London and a little over. Over what? He did not ask. He went down the stair in his stockinged feet, past the cat that looked at him with the comet's own eye, and let himself out into the street.

The night train out of Stoke was empty but for a drover asleep over his stick and a woman with a basket that smelt of fish. Leadford sat in the corner with his face against the glass, and England went by him backward, black hedge and black field, a farmhouse lamp, a level crossing with a lantern like a held breath. Stafford. Rugby, where the platform lamps swung and a porter called the name as though it were a charge against someone. The drover snored. The wheels said it over and over, and he could not tell whether they said Nettie or Verrall or Received with thanks, so that all three ran together into one rhythm of iron: nettieverrallthanks, nettieverrallthanks.

He took out the parcel at Rugby and undid the little bow and looked at the revolver in the lamplight. Dull blue. Not so large as he had thought. A small thing to carry so much. He loaded it as the pawnbroker had shown him, thumb and click, six chambers, six small rooms for six small tenants, and it came to him with a queer lightness that he did not know to what end. Not truly. Only that the direction was London and the hour was now.

He put the window down an inch. Cold air and the smell of coal and wet fields. And the sky, east and south, had changed again. The stars were thinning in it as sugar thins in tea. Over the ridge the comet rode, immense now, a head of pale fire with a tail swept half across heaven, and the whole vault was tinted with a faint and delicate green, the green of a leaf held up against a candle, the green of the first morning of the first world. It lay on the fields. It lay on the hands in his lap. It lay on the revolver and made of it a toy.

Strange, he thought, how quiet. As if the earth had stopped to listen. As if something enormous, kind and indifferent, were leaning down to look at William Leadford and had not yet decided.

—Euston, the drover muttered in his sleep. Euston. Fare thee well.

The train ran on into the green.
